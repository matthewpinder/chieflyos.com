On your computer

A private AI assistant that runs on your computer.

Chiefly is a private AI assistant that runs on your computer. That sentence is the claim. Private means you can point at the machine it lives on. It runs there. Local by default, cloud opt-in.

Where “private” has to land

A lot of software uses the word private for a policy. A policy can be rewritten. A computer you own is a place. Chiefly starts on that computer. Remembering what matters, and handling the boring stuff, is meant to happen there. If a task needs a cloud model, you opt in. The cloud is not the quiet default hiding under the word private.

Local by default is something you can check. The assistant is on your computer when you have not asked for the cloud. The goals you want kept in sight, and the record of what it did, live with the copy you have. You can see and delete what it remembers, anytime. Seeing it is part of the product. Deleting it is part of the product. You do not file a request and wait for someone else to agree.

Cloud is a choice, not the starting point

Cloud opt-in has to stay in the same sentence as local by default. Some work fits a larger model than the one on your machine. Some work needs a service. Chiefly lets you choose that. Choosing it means the task leaves the computer, because that is what a cloud model is. Not choosing it means the task stays. A private AI assistant that runs on your computer is still Chiefly when the cloud is off.

Offline is the same fact on a day with no network. With a local model, Chiefly can work while you are offline. A local model runs on your computer. It does not need to send the prompt away to answer. The cloud path does need a network. Those are two ways to use it, and this page will not blur them. If you opted in, the cloud part waits for a connection. The local part does not.

It still asks before it acts

Chiefly is a personal chief of staff. It remembers what matters, handles the boring stuff, and leaves the day clear. It is not a promise that the day becomes empty. It holds the small things so you can see the day. It asks before it acts. Nothing happens until you say so. A message is not sent on its own. A change is not made on its own. A spend is not made on its own.

Spending waits for your PIN. An approval is you saying yes. A refusal means nothing was sent. All clear. The longer version of that rule is on It asks before it acts. The short version is the same rule: an assistant that can act is only yours if it stops at the action.

Memory you can see, memory you can delete, and actions that wait are one idea. You are in charge of what it holds and what it does next. An assistant that is private about storage and careless about sending would still be acting past you.

You pay once

Chiefly is not a subscription. There is no monthly fee for the copy on your computer. You pay once, and your Chiefly keeps working, even if we don’t. Updates may stop; your copy keeps working. The local copy does not need our company to stay switched on in order to remain the tool you have. Cloud features you opted into are the part that needs a service. The copy on the computer is the part that does not.

What it will not do to your attention

Chiefly is coming to Mac and Windows. No nudges unless you set them. A nudge is a tap for your attention. An approval is a stop before an action. Those are different. Nudges stay off unless you ask for them. Approvals stay, because nothing should happen until you say so.

The longer philosophy is the paper, which is with a professor for review and is not public yet. The short version is the product itself: a private AI assistant that runs on your computer, local by default, cloud opt-in, and quiet until you approve.
