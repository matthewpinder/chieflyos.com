The boundary

It asks before it acts.

Chiefly asks before it acts. Nothing happens until you say so. That is the rule. This page is the plain version: approvals, a PIN when money would move, and a stop if you do not agree.

A reply is not an action

An assistant that only talks can be wrong in a reply, and the reply sits there until you use it. An assistant that can act is different. Sending, changing, and spending are not replies. They leave your hands. They can reach other people, or they can touch your money. Chiefly treats those as actions. An action waits.

The wait is an approval. Chiefly can propose. A proposal is a suggestion: here is what it would do. You accept it or you refuse it. Accepting is you saying so. Refusing means the action does not happen. You are not browsing a log of things it already did. You are looking at something that has not happened yet.

What a refusal looks like

The homepage shows the shape of that moment. An invite conflicts with a Friday 4 PM focus block. Chiefly suggests Tuesday at 11. You refuse. Nothing is sent. All clear. The example is there so the rule is visible, not so the product can claim a clever calendar trick. The assistant does not get to decide that a conflict is yours to fix by sending something.

All clear is the state after a refusal, and it is the state when nothing is waiting on you. It is not a claim that the proposal was wise. It is a claim that nothing left your hands. If the suggestion was bad, the bad idea stopped at the suggestion. You can refuse a good idea too. The rule does not grade your reasons.

A PIN when it would spend

Spending has one more lock. A spend waits for your PIN. The PIN is how you approve money. Chiefly does not spend because a plan looked reasonable, and it does not spend because a deadline was close. It stops and asks for the PIN. Until you enter it, nothing is spent.

The PIN is yours. Chiefly does not take a cut of what you spend. If you approve a spend, that money is your decision about your money. The approval is not a fee, and it is not a share. It is a stop, and then a yes or a no from you.

Sending waits the same way

Posting and sending sit under the same rule as spending. Nothing is posted and nothing is sent until you say so. A draft can exist. A draft is not the action. The action is the send. The send waits. If you are not sure, you refuse, and the result is the same line: nothing sent, all clear.

This is not a promise that every proposal will be right. Chiefly can suggest the wrong time, the wrong person, or the wrong amount. The safeguard is not perfection. The safeguard is that a wrong proposal is still only a proposal. You are the one who turns it into an action. If you want a tool that just does it, this is the wrong tool. Chiefly is built to stop.

Approvals are not nudges

No nudges unless you set them. A nudge is a tap for your attention when you did not ask. Chiefly does not do that unless you set the nudges. An approval is not a nudge. It appears because an action would happen, and the action is not allowed to happen alone. You are not signed up for a stream of check-ins. You are asked when silence would mean the software acted for you.

The memory behind a proposal is on your computer. Local by default, cloud opt-in. You can see and delete what it remembers, anytime. An assistant that asks permission and hides what it knows would still be a closed box. You can open what it holds. You can empty it. The longer page on where that lives is the private AI assistant.

The copy that asks is the copy you keep

You pay once. Chiefly is never a subscription. You pay once, and your Chiefly keeps working, even if we don’t. Updates may stop; your copy keeps working. The rule about asking does not depend on a server staying up. The copy on your computer is the copy that asks. Cloud, if you opt in, is a choice on top. The local copy keeps the boundary.
