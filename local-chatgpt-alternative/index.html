A different approach

A ChatGPT alternative that runs locally.

People look for a ChatGPT alternative that runs locally when they want an assistant on their own computer, not only a chat window in a browser. Chiefly is that kind of tool. It runs on your computer. Local by default, cloud opt-in.

This page compares approaches. It is not a complaint about a chat product. A chat assistant is a real way to work. You open it, you type, it answers. That is useful when you have a question. Chiefly is built for a different job, and it lives in a different place. You do not have to dislike one to want the other.

Where the assistant runs

A chat assistant, the kind people mean when they say ChatGPT, is a conversation you hold through an account. The model runs on a service. Your words go there, and the reply comes back. You are visiting the assistant. The assistant is not a copy sitting on your machine.

Chiefly starts the other way. It is a personal chief of staff that runs on your computer. Local by default means it is on your machine unless you say otherwise. A local model runs there, including when you are offline. Cloud opt-in means you can choose a cloud model for a task. That choice sends the task off the machine, because a cloud model is not local. If you do not opt in, the task stays.

Both approaches can sit on the same desk. One is a visit to a service. One is a tool you keep. The difference is the place the work happens, not a ranking of which brand is kinder. If you want the help on your computer, the local approach is the one that matches. The page for that claim, without the comparison, is a private AI assistant that runs on your computer.

A reply, or a step that waits

A chat box answers when you type. You drive each turn, and the answer stays an answer until you use it. Chiefly can look at what you said matters and propose a next step. Then it waits. Nothing happens until you say so. It does not send, change, or spend on its own.

If the proposal is wrong, you refuse it. Nothing is sent. All clear. If money would move, it waits for your PIN. The approval is the product. A draft can exist without becoming an action. Sending is the action, and sending waits. The plain version of that rule is it asks before it acts.

This is a different interaction, not a claim that chat is careless. Chat is built to answer. Chiefly is built to stop before it acts. If you want something that just does the thing, Chiefly is the wrong shape. If you want a proposal you can refuse, it is the right one.

Renting a seat, or keeping a copy

Many assistants are offered as a subscription. You pay for as long as you want to keep the seat. That can be a fair shape for a service that runs on someone else’s computers. It is not the shape Chiefly uses. Chiefly is a one-time payment, never a subscription.

You pay once, and your Chiefly keeps working, even if we don’t. Updates may stop; your copy keeps working. The copy on your computer does not need our servers to remain your tool. Cloud features, if you opted into them, need a service. The local copy does not. A subscription ending and a local copy you already have are not the same event.

What it remembers stays where you can inspect it. You can see and delete what it remembers, anytime. A local assistant you cannot open would only be local in the description. Seeing the memory, and deleting it, is how the privacy claim stays about your computer.

If you want a ChatGPT alternative that runs locally, the checks are ordinary. Does it run on your computer? Can you leave the cloud off? Does it wait before it acts? Can you pay once and keep the copy? Chiefly is built so those answers are yes. A chat assistant you visit in a browser is built so the work happens on a service. That is a fair description of the approach, and it is enough of a comparison.
